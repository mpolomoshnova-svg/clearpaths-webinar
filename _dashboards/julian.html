03.C — Working-Style Note

Gifted + ADHD changes the strategy more than most consultants will admit.

Gifted + ADHD ("twice exceptional") students typically respond poorly to volume-based prep — more APs, more activities, more pressure. They thrive on interest-anchored intensity: one project they care about deeply, with structured external accountability.

Paralysis under stress is the strongest argument against a "load up the resume" approach. It also makes timeline-driven tasks (essays, applications) the highest-risk parts of the process. We plan around this from day one.

The "engineering + design + ceramics" interest is the natural lever for intrinsic motivation. Building a profile around it isn't just admissions strategy — it's the only path that won't break him in junior year.

Fig. 05 — On "Not Looking Overly Privileged"

You named the worry. Here's what defuses it.

This is a real concern at UCLA / Berkeley / private competitive schools. The pattern that triggers it vs the pattern that defuses it:

Triggers the read

  • Multi-week summer programs at brand-name universities ($8–15K) with no follow-on
  • International "service trips" with no local equivalent
  • Expensive sports + instrument + tutoring + prep — all at once
  • No paid work, no entrepreneurial work, no scrappy work

Defuses the read

  • Self-launched projects (built or made something, even small)
  • Local engagement (school, neighborhood, community)
  • Sustained depth in one or two areas, not breadth in many
  • Visible work product — portfolio, GitHub, Etsy, exhibition
  • Some form of work or service that involves discomfort, not just achievement
For Julian: a maker portfolio beats a fancy summer program. A small Etsy ceramics shop beats a $12K STEM camp. A GitHub repo beats a college-name lanyard.

Fig. 07 — Why Now

Three real deadlines, all in the next eight weeks.

  1. Junior year course requests close in May / early June. A junior year that includes a design / engineering elective vs one that doesn't is the single most-watched signal on his transcript. This decision is made in the next four weeks and cannot be undone in fall.
