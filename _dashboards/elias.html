Your Question, Answered

How we actually help younger students discover passions.

We do not "give" 9th graders passions. No consultant can. What we do is structural.

  1. Map the surface area.

    What's he actually drawn to when nobody is grading him? Where does he lose track of time? What does he choose when he's bored? This is a 4-week structured exercise, not a survey.

  2. Force exposure.

    Most kids don't know what a research economist does, or what an industrial designer does, or what a data journalist does, because nobody told them. We curate exposure across 8–12 fields in 9th grade.

  3. Run small experiments.

    A 4-week project is not a passion — it's a test. We run 3–4 of them in 9th and 10th grade. The ones that survive are real. The ones that die were curiosity, not direction.

  4. Watch what survives 6 months.

    Real interests have inertia. If he's still doing it in February when nobody is making him, that's the spike.

  5. Then double down.

    From 11th grade on, the work is depth — leadership, projects, public output, mentorship — not breadth.

This process is impossible to start in junior year. It's why families with juniors and seniors are paying $15K+ to construct retroactively what 9th-grade families build organically. You're starting at exactly the right time.

Why The Next 60 Days Matter

Three reasons this is not a "you can wait" situation — even with four years of runway.

  1. Math placement closes by mid-summer at most California schools. Once his 9th-grade math is set, every subsequent year of math is determined by it. Top targets like UCLA / Berkeley / Brown / Northwestern want students who reach AP Calc by senior year minimum; the strongest applicants reach it by junior year.
  2. High school identity calcifies in 9th grade. Whether he's "the baseball kid," "the artistic kid," or "the smart kid" gets decided socially in the first six weeks of 9th grade — and that identity shapes which clubs he joins, who his friends are, and which opportunities he pursues. We can be intentional about this.
  3. The interest-discovery process takes six months minimum. If we want to know by end of 9th grade what direction he's pulling toward, we have to start the structured exercises this summer. Starting in 10th grade compresses the runway by half.
This is the lowest-stress, highest-leverage moment in his entire admissions arc. The window is real, and it's narrow at the front end.
