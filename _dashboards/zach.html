Your Specific Questions

Two questions, addressed directly.

Will his 1:1 private school be viewed as a handicap?

Short answer: No, if framed correctly. Yes, if not addressed.

Admissions readers at competitive schools see "non-traditional school" and look for context. The story you've already articulated — discipline-based ADHD management, recovery from injury without surgery, flexibility for elite athletic training — is genuinely compelling. It becomes an asset when:

  • The school counselor letter explicitly addresses the school's structure and rigor.
  • The Common App "Additional Information" section explains the academic environment (courses available, grading scale).
  • One Personal Insight or supplemental essay touches on the agency the environment gave him.

It becomes a problem if the application doesn't address it and reviewers fill in the gaps themselves.

Why Now

This isn't "you should start sometime." It's three real deadlines compressed into one summer.

  1. June 15, 2026 — D1 coaches can begin contacting him. Coaches build their watchlists in May. If his name and film aren't in front of them by then, he loses six months of momentum.
  2. Junior year course requests are due to school counselors in May / early June. A 4-AP junior year vs a 2-AP junior year is decided in the next four weeks. Unfixable in the fall.
  3. The next three months are when he can launch a passion project that shows 18 months of depth. Starting in junior fall = 6 months of depth — visibly thinner.
Every week of delay cuts the runway, not the workload.
